What we built

02Two operations, one way of working2:00 · 3 min
“A customer with a mobile line and a fibre line is one customer. Until this month, the teams looking after them used two tools, two alert queues, and two different definitions of the word ‘working’.”
  • Mobile side: 21 journeys — activation, port-in, identity, payments, delivery. 62 rules.
  • Fixed side: four digital channels — web, the Salam Home app, the dealer map, QR referrals. 115 rules.
  • The part that matters commercially is the shared spine: Customer 360, the AI, alerting, objectives, exports, audit, roles — built once.
  • Say it plainly: “the second business cost a fraction of the first, and the third will cost less again.”
If someone asks “why not buy a tool?” — answer here, briefly: no product on the market knows a Salam journey. We would have paid for a licence and still built the mapping ourselves.
Bridge: “So what does leadership actually get out of it? Four questions.”
03Four questions, four screens5:00 · 4 min
“Every one of these four screens is a question I used to answer with a report, a week later, from a spreadsheet somebody built by hand.”
  • Is the business healthy? — orders, activations, payments, delivery in one view.
  • What is broken and who is on it? — severity, age, owner, and the runbook attached.
  • Where do customers give up? — the step, with its drop-off and its error.
  • Is it us or a partner? — the actual request and response. Evidence, not opinion.
  • Slow down on the last one. “Evidence, not opinion” is what changes a vendor conversation.
Bridge: “Let me take the third question and show you what it looks like with real numbers.”
04Where customers actually stop9:00 · 3 min
“A journey is not up or down. It is a place where customers stop — and until you can name the step, you are guessing.”
  • Walk down the funnel slowly. Let the shape do the work.
  • The headline: roughly half of everyone who leaves, leaves at the very first form. That is a product decision, not an incident.
  • Under 1% are lost at payment — so the instinct to blame the payment rail is usually wrong.
  • Land it: “‘sales are down’ becomes ‘one step in one channel lost eleven points this week, and here are the sessions’.”
  • The argument stops being whether there is a problem, and starts being which team owns the step.
Bridge: “That only works if the alerts are trusted. Which is where we spent this month.”

Why it is trusted

05Alerting that earns attention12:00 · 3.5 min
“An alert that fires on ordinary traffic is worse than no alert at all. It teaches the team to ignore the screen.”
  • 177 rules across both businesses. Not one of them fires without a written next step. Say that twice — it is the discipline, not the count, that matters.
  • We measured, for every rule, how often its own condition was already true. A rule that is true 30% of the time is not detecting an incident — it is describing a normal Tuesday.
  • Result of the tuning: open Fixed incidents went from 14 to 1. The rules did not get weaker.
  • One signal now raises one incident, at the worst severity seen. Before, a single outage could page three people from three queues.
  • 16 minutes to acknowledge, 3.9 hours to resolve — measured from detection, not from when someone noticed.

06Service objectives15:30 · 2.5 min
“Before this, whether a journey was healthy got settled in the meeting after the complaint — by whoever had the better anecdote.”
  • Each journey now carries its target, its warning band, and the window it is measured over.
  • The target and the alarm read the same measurement, so they can never drift apart.
  • The real shift: “not ‘is this bad?’ but ‘we are inside the objective, and here is how much budget is left before we are not’.”
  • Vendors are held to the same numbers we hold ourselves to.

07AI inside operations18:00 · 3 min
“Three models, running on our own infrastructure, and customer data is masked before anything reaches them. No operational data leaves Salam to be answered.”
  • Yusr: type a number, an OTP, a reference — and just ask. No query to write, no second person to call.
  • Assisted triage: it reads the failure evidence and proposes a classification and a likely cause, so L1 start from a hypothesis rather than a blank page.
  • Say the governance line unprompted, before anyone asks: “it decides nothing.” It proposes and attaches its reasoning; an engineer accepts or rejects, and that acceptance is the record.
  • Close: “automation finds it, AI explains it, the accountability stays with the engineer.”
Bridge: “Let me show you what that is worth on a real week — last week.”

The proof

08The worked example21:00 · 4 min
“Last week a report landed showing one sales channel at roughly half the previous month. It arrived as a suspected technical fault, with a request to investigate.”
  • Tell it as a story, in order. Pause after the escalation before giving the answer.
  • Per day the two periods were 33.1 against 33.0 — identical. The 45% gap was seventeen days measured against thirty-one.
  • The channel was in fact at its highest rolling average of the year.
  • We did not stop there. One genuine dip remained — but traffic was up 31% while orders were up 8%, every provider and every partner moved together, and no step in the funnel changed. Demand and mix, not a failure.
  • Before: a week of cross-team investigation, three teams off delivery work, and probably a conclusion nobody could evidence.
  • Instead: one read, one query, answered the same day with the funnel, the providers and the partners all shown.
“The value is not that we were right. It is that being right took hours, and came with the evidence attached.”
Do not name anyone. If asked who raised it: “a business report — and the right instinct, it just turned out to be arithmetic.” Protect the person who escalated.
Bridge: “The same discipline now covers the regulator.”

10Access, PII and audit27:00 · 2.5 min
“Everyone sees exactly their job. Nothing more — and it is on the record.”
  • 24 pages, 14 roles, one model. No page has a private rule of its own any more.
  • Customer data is masked by default. Revealing a value is a deliberate act, never a mode a session sits in — and every reveal is written with the person, the record and the time.
  • A Mobile account never renders a Fixed page, and the reverse.
  • User administration answers to the account tier in code — no permission setting can open it.

If they ask

“What did it cost?”
Built in-house by the existing team alongside delivery work. No licence, no vendor contract, no external professional services. The cost is our people's time — and the alternative was paying for a product that does not know a Salam journey and building the mapping anyway.
“What happens if you leave?”
Fair question, and the honest answer is it is a risk we are actively reducing. Everything is documented in-repo, the runbooks sit on the rules themselves rather than in someone's head, and the next phase is about making the platform operable by the team rather than by its authors. That is part of what I am asking you to back.
“Why not buy an observability product?”
The market products watch infrastructure — CPU, latency, errors. We needed something that watches a Salam journey: a port-in, a Nafath check, an SDA dealer order, a QR referral. No vendor ships that. We would have paid for a licence and then built exactly this on top of it.
“Is customer data safe in an AI system?”
Inference runs on our own infrastructure — nothing leaves Salam. Data is masked before it reaches a model. And the model proposes; it never decides or acts. Every reveal of a real value is a deliberate, audited action by a named person.
“How do I know these numbers are real?”
Every figure on these slides comes out of the running platform, and I can open it live in this room. That is rather the point of the whole thing.
“What is still missing?”
Answer this one straight, it buys credibility. Assisted resolution is not built yet. ServiceNow is not integrated yet. One Fixed data feed needs an owner. And coverage of the Fixed side is newer than Mobile, so it will need tuning for another month. None of that is a reason to wait to make it official.
